# System Audit — what the game actually is, as of 2026-08-12

*Produced by reading the CODE, not the design docs. Ten parallel readers, one per subsystem,
then a synthesis pass for where the systems fight each other. Every number is cited to file:line
in the raw output at `production/audit/systems-map.json`.*

This exists because the flow redesign needs a truthful starting point, and design intent and
implementation have diverged far enough that the GDDs are no longer a description of the game.

## Per-system

### Core combat loop and wave/depth progression

In play this is a fully automatic, zero-input wave treadmill: one champion fights an identical enemy sprite over and over in a fixed arena, every 5th wave is that region's boss, each cleared wave instantly pays gleam (+cores/chest on bosses), and when the champion dies it respawns at full HP and restarts from wave 1 with nothing lost. The player never touches the fight — the ONLY interactive element on the combat screen is a 4-button BATTLE SPEED selector (x1/x2/x4/x8) (`SoloExpeditionScreen.cs:866-877`); everything else is decided in other screens (Build/Mastery/Stats/Gear/Map) and read passively here. The fight itself resolves instantly in one call (`SoloExpedition.PushWave`) and is then *replayed* as animation; reward crediting is gated on that animation finishing, so the speed button is a real ~2x throughput lever, not cosmetic. Run length is decided almost entirely by HP attrition against a compounding enemy-damage curve, and because the boss's 2.2x scale is applied to enemy DAMAGE as well as health (`SoloExpedition.cs:136`), 20 of 21 simulated runs across the whole training range end on a boss wave — depth is quantized to multiples of 5 and long stretches of stat investment produce literally zero depth change. Design intent and code have fully diverged: `combat-encounter-system.md` (1303 lines: weak-point targeting, dodge/block/interrupt, Resonance meter, ultimates) and `expedition-auto-battle.md` (5-creature squad, bank-or-push priced exit) describe systems with zero implementation in `src/`.

**Real choice available:** Effectively zero in the moment and shallow out of it: the only in-fight input is a speed button, and off-fight the build space collapses because ~31 of 43 mastery nodes, 15 of 44 dust nodes, and 5 of 13 keystones are flat multipliers, the Source pick has one correct answer per region, VOW OF PATIENCE is a free 1.6x after 10 seconds, and the whole Rarity channel is disconnected.

**Designed but not reachable:**
- `BuildMods.Rarity` → `Haul.Quality` is computed (`SoloExpedition.cs:191`) and NEVER read. `Game1.cs:1238-1252` consumes only `Haul.Gleam` and `Haul.Cores`. Chest loot quality comes from the chest's own rarity (`Chest.cs:184,192`), not the build. Consequence: FORTUNE (Rarity x2, damage x0.75) and DISCERNING EYE (Rarity x2, haul x0.5) are strictly negative keystones; the `fortune_1/fortune_2/ks_fortune` dust road (105 dust) buys nothing.
- SPLINTER is dead the same way — `SoloBattle.cs:188` adds +0.15 quality per kill into the same discarded channel. REAPER (grants Splinter, costs -25% skill rate) is therefore a pure downside. `TriggerLivenessTests.cs:295` asserts only that the number lands in the bonus object, not that it reaches loot.
- AURA completely ignores `mods.SkillRate` — its tick gate is `ms % AuraTickMs` with no rate divisor (`SoloBattle.cs:274-279`). Consequence: TITAN (Health x3, SkillRate x0.4) is a cost-free x3 health upgrade for an Aura build. MEASURED: 4x Aura goes 14 → 19 depth with TITAN, while 4x Projectile goes 19 → 14 with the same keystone.
- BLOODLUST and ZEAL stack multiplicatively (`SoloBattle.cs:207-221`). Taking the Bloodlust keystone (or the free `strike_n` mastery notable, `MasteryTree.cs:83`) together with JUGGERNAUT guarantees a minimum 1.8x damage at any health fraction (1.96x at 50%). JUGGERNAUT carries `BuildMods.None` — its only cost is NoHealing (`Keystones.cs:103-106`).
- VOW OF PATIENCE (`ResonanceWeaving.cs:282-287`) triggers on expedition-ABSOLUTE elapsed ms ≥ 10,000, which is monotonic across waves — so it latches permanently on from ~wave 7 and pays 1.6x for free. It strictly dominates VOW OF THE UNBROKEN (same 1.6x, conditional on HP) and out-earns both static-cost vows (1.333x and 1.45x, both with real costs). It is also the FIRST vow the dust tree teaches (`vow_study_1`, 15 dust).
- The boss's 2.2x is applied to enemy DAMAGE as well as health (`SoloExpedition.cs:136` passes the same `scale` to both parameters). Undocumented — `WaveModel.cs:25` says 'a boss's health on top of the wave's own scaling'. This single line is what quantizes all progression to 5-wave steps.

### Hunter levelling, stats, training and power

Hunter levelling/stats/training is a fully-built system with no reachable entry point. The nine-stat catalog (`HunterProgression.cs:9-13`), the geometric Gleam training curve, the 60-rank cap, the save round-trip and ~12 unit tests all exist and work — but the only method that writes a rank, `Hunter.Train()` (`HunterProgression.cs:284`), is called from exactly three places: two `RH_SHOT_MODE` screenshot fixtures (`Game1.cs:761-762,772-775`) and save-restore (`SaveGame.cs:447`). The STATS screen that used to own training was rebuilt read-only on 2026-07-28 (`StatsScreen.cs:60` — `Update()` is an empty body; commit `c4319bd` says so in its own message: "stat TRAINING (hunter.Train) is no longer reachable — flagged for the owner"). Consequence for the live player: all nine stats are permanently frozen at their base values, HUNTER LEVEL is permanently 1 (`HunterProgression.cs:106` — level is `1 + totalRanks/5`, and totalRanks is always 0), and the nav legend still advertises "STATS — TRAIN" (`Game1.cs:1783`). What the player actually experiences under the "hunter power" label is a gear-only curve: `PowerRating` (`Gear.cs:113-114`) is a 3-term arithmetic sum that reads the weapon multiplier, the charm, and Defense affixes — and ignores every trait, every Damage/Health/SkillRate/Haul/Crit affix, and six of the eight equipment slots.

**Real choice available:** Zero — there is no reachable choice; the nine stats are read-only constants and the only progression the player can act on is which item goes in which of eight slots.

**Designed but not reachable:**
- TRAINING IS UNREACHABLE. `Hunter.Train()` has no player-facing caller. `StatsScreen.Update()` is an empty method body (`StatsScreen.cs:60`), and the class doc admits it: "stat TRAINING (hunter.Train) is intentionally not here — flagged for the owner, since it currently has no other home" (`StatsScreen.cs:22-23`). Commit `c4319bd` deleted the working train UI (rows with `TrainBtn`, `TryTrain`, "RANK n / 60", "NEXT RANK COSTS n GLEAM", and a live DPS bench) to conform to a UX spec that said the screen should be read-only.
- Because of the above, ALL NINE STATS ARE FROZEN and HUNTER LEVEL IS PERMANENTLY 1 in normal play, while four screens display it as if it moves.
- The nav legend still reads `("V", "STATS — TRAIN")` (`Game1.cs:1783`) and the keybind comment still reads "Commander training, spelled out, on its own page" (`Game1.cs:1024`). Both advertise a feature that no longer exists.
- `DamageBench` (`Builds/DamageBench.cs`) — a live-sim DPS bench explicitly built "so a stat that quietly reaches no formula shows up plainly as 'trained it, the number did not budge'" — has zero call sites outside tests. The instrument built to detect this exact bug class was removed from the UI in the same commit that caused it.
- ~12 unit tests (`HunterStatWiringTests.cs`, `LootSystemTests.cs:161-190,242-272`, `DamageBenchTests.cs:59`, `SaveSystemTests.cs:20-21`, `FullLoopTests.cs:64-65`) all call `Train()` and all pass. The suite guards a system the player cannot touch, so CI is green on a dead feature.
- 79,556 Gleam of designed sink (`HunterProgression.cs:324-328`) was deleted from the economy without replacement. The GDD's entire §4.5 proof ("8.2–21.2 hours to absorb the sink") is void; remaining Gleam sinks are Refine and Warren upgrades only.

### Skills / builds: Source x Form x Vow weaving and the mastery tree

A "build" is one editable loadout object (`PlayerLoadout`) with three parts: 4 skill slots, each a (Source × Form × Vow) triple cycled with on-screen arrows; up to 3 keystone sockets filled from whatever the Memory Dust tree has taught; and a 42-node mastery tree that is fully completable and freely respeccable. The fight is 100% automatic — nothing in this system is exercised during combat, only configured before it, and the player never sees a build number that reflects any of it. Of the three weaving axes only Form actually changes how combat resolves (six genuinely different verbs in `FormBehaviour`/`SoloBattle`); Source is a single 1.5/1.0/0.667 lookup against the region's element, and Vow is a per-skill damage multiplier with a price. The mastery tree's only real content is 6 trigger-granting notables — the other 36 nodes are flat percentages — and because earned points exceed node count by mid-game, every finished character ends with all 6 notables and an identical stat block, differing only by which of 6 affinity corners they took (and that is a free, instant toggle).

**Real choice available:** Two real decisions (which Form you spam, and whether a keystone's downside is worth it) buried under ~70 free-take percentage nodes — and a strictly-correct opening exists and costs 3 points: morph_1 → morph_2 → morph_n grants ECHO (every skill fires twice, zero cost) on turn one.

**Designed but not reachable:**
- BuildMods.Rarity is completely inert. It terminates in `Haul.Quality` (SoloExpedition.cs:191) and no consumer ever reads that field — Game1.cs:1235-1252 takes only Gleam and Cores. So FORTUNE (Rarity 2.0 / Damage 0.75, Keystones.cs:113), DISCERNING EYE (Rarity 2.0 / Haul 0.5, Keystones.cs:74), the fortune_1/fortune_2 dust nodes (+8% each, MemoryDust.cs:346-349), and SPLINTER's AddQuality(0.15) (SoloBattle.cs:189) are pure downside. REAPER pays SkillRate 0.75 for a trigger whose only effect is that dead quality bump (Keystones.cs:80-81).
- Only 3 keystone chips are ever drawn or hit-tested (BuildScreen.cs:171 and 421, both `i < learned.Count && i < 3`), with no scrolling. 13 keystones are learnable. Once a 4th is learned it is permanently unreachable — which deletes the exact refusal DustEffects.cs:52-55 calls the point of the system ('the menu is deliberately longer than the plate').
- VOW OF PATIENCE is a free +60% damage. Its `AfterElapsedMs` threshold is checked against the expedition-ABSOLUTE clock (SoloBattle.cs:419 passes `absMs`; Champion.ElapsedMs accumulates across waves at SoloBattle.cs:179), so after the first 10 seconds of a run it is permanently true — yet it is priced at 0.6 uptime for 1.6×, with no cost of any kind, on all four slots simultaneously. It is also the FIRST vow taught (15 dust). This is precisely the never-false failure the file's own comments say was already fixed twice (ResonanceWeaving.cs:50-57 and 232-247).
- Mastery notables grant triggers FREE (MasteryTree.cs:153 passes BuildMods.None) that keystones charge for. morph_n grants Echo — 2× casts, no penalty — where the ECHO keystone charges Damage 0.6 (Keystones.cs:61); strike_n grants Bloodlust free where the BLOODLUST keystone charges Health 0.75 (Keystones.cs:54). Triggers are a HashSet (Build.cs:307), so socketing the keystone after taking the notable is a pure, silent penalty with zero benefit.
- Nothing prevents taking ALL SIX notables. `CanTake` only blocks a second Mastery corner (MasteryTree.cs:209); the notables cost 3 points each and points exceed nodes by mid-game. Every finished character therefore carries Bloodlust + Overdraw + Radiance + Desperation + Linger + Echo simultaneously, and the 'which arm are you' question the tree's own doc comment poses (MasteryTree.cs:49-56) never gets asked.
- LINGER makes MARK a permanent flat 1.6× damage stat: window 2500×9/5 = 4500ms (SoloBattle.cs:293) exceeds Mark's 4000ms cooldown (FormBehaviour.cs:51). This directly contradicts the rule written 20 lines above it ('an amplifier that is always up is not an amplifier, it is a damage stat', FormBehaviour.cs:71-76). Even without LINGER, Mark goes permanent once mods.SkillRate ≥ 1.6 — a full mastery tree (1.52) × full dust tree (1.26) = 1.92 gets there on its own.

### Memory Dust prestige tree — what it resets, what it grants, how it is earned

Memory Dust is not a prestige system in play — it resets nothing, has no "prestige" button, no confirmation, no run boundary, and no respec. It is a second passive-skill-tree currency that trickles in passively (overwhelmingly from Warren facilities, not from the "region mastery" faucet the code comments claim) and is spent, permanently, on a flat 44-node shopping grid worth 1,775 Dust total. Of those 44 nodes, 15 are small flat multipliers (the whole tree bought = +36% dmg / +33% HP / +26% skill rate / +33% haul), 13 are gates that teach a keystone, 5 teach Vows, and the remaining 11 are rate tweaks, two loot filters, and three nodes with no gameplay consumer at all. The only nodes that change how combat resolves are the 13 keystone gates and the 5 Vow gates — and the player can only ever wear 3 keystones, which is the one real decision the system contains. The screen presents this as a scrollable card grid sorted by category then cost, never as a graph, so the branch-vs-branch tension the catalog was authored around is invisible to the player.

**Real choice available:** Two real choices exist and neither lives on this screen: which 3 of 13 keystones to wear, and which Vow to hang on each skill — the other 31 nodes are a flat, strictly-correct shopping list you finish in ~3 minutes of idle time.

**Designed but not reachable:**
- BuildMods.Rarity is computed and thrown away. SoloExpedition.cs:191 folds it into Haul.Quality, Game1.cs:1236-1240 reads only Haul.Gleam and Haul.Cores, and DropBossChest (Game1.cs:1296-1308) rolls loot tier from region ladder / progression / depth / corruption while ignoring Haul.Quality entirely; Chest.cs:184 derives quality from the chest's own rarity instead. Consequence: fortune_1 + fortune_2 (45 Dust) do nothing, and two 60-Dust keystones are pure downside — FORTUNE is -25% damage for nothing (Keystones.cs:113) and DISCERNING EYE is -50% haul for nothing (Keystones.cs:74).
- All 4 SHARPENED RECALL nodes (130 Dust) are dead on a fresh save. MasteryRate only enters RegionAutomation.Tick (RegionAutomation.cs:230), and Game1.cs:529 and 419 both skip any region with Team.Count == 0. The creature-assignment UI is retired (Game1.cs:147-148 comment; Game1.cs:1516 routes the A key to the Warren dashboard instead), so no farm is ever staffed. The one surviving RMP source, RecordActiveKill (RegionAutomation.cs:186), is a flat +5 that masteryRate never touches.
- Three nodes have zero game-code consumers. DustEffects.ShowExactNumbers (ledger, 20 Dust), ShowMergePreview (forge_insight, 25 Dust) and TreeComplete (attunement, 45 Dust) are called only from tests (DustEffectsTests.cs:267-285). Their descriptions were already reworded to 'GROUNDWORK' / 'OPENS THE...' to stop lying, but attunement's 'A QUIET MARK THAT YOU HAVE SEEN ALL OF IT' (MemoryDust.cs:360) marks nothing — nor is MemoryDustTree.IsComplete (MemoryDust.cs:147) read anywhere in src, despite the doc comment promising 'a player deserves to KNOW when they are finished'.
- The tree's own wiring test cannot catch the above. DustEffects.WiredIds (DustEffects.cs:194-200) auto-passes every node that merely carries a Mods or GrantsKeystone field, and the rest come from a hand-maintained NamedWires set (DustEffects.cs:185-192). A node is 'wired' if DustEffects reads it — not if anything downstream of DustEffects does.
- The keystone refusal is designed but not presented. Build.KeystoneSlots = 3 of 13 learnable, and Build.cs:204-222 argues at length that this is the point — but BuildScreen.cs:171 and 420 both loop 'i < learned.Count && i < 3', so only the first three keystones in catalog order are ever drawn or clickable. Buy the fourth keystone gate and you cannot reach it.
- The Warren silently replaced the designed faucet. MemoryDust.cs:95-97 states 'This is the ONLY faucet: Dust comes from mastering regions', and MemoryDustTests.cs:58 asserts a test named test_the_only_way_to_gain_dust_is_mastery. In the shipped game, mastery + conquest + corruption together yield a few hundred Dust over the whole campaign while FORAGING PITS alone pays 450/min from the first second — roughly 99.9% of income arrives through AwardFromMastery calls that have nothing to do with mastery (Game1.cs:393, 543).

### Items: slots, rarity, traits, affixes, enchants, upgrade and merge

In play, "items" is: watch the auto-battler, wait for a boss wave (every 5th) to maybe drop a chest (30–60%), press OPEN, get 1–2 pieces, then decide equip / sell / dismantle / merge / refine / reforge in the Forge. An item is 5 rolled facts — rarity, item level, element, one trait, one enchant (Rare+) — plus 0–4 affixes, and all of trait/enchant/affixes are pure FNV-1a hashes of the InstanceId, so a drop is completely frozen at mint and the only way to change it is to pay materials to re-roll (Reforge) or destroy it (Merge, which re-hashes everything because it mints a new id). Rarity is the dominant axis by a wide margin: it multiplies the weapon damage bonus (0.20 → 7.00, a 35× spread), sets affix COUNT, gates enchants entirely, and scales every trait's upside. The only genuinely build-shaping content is the 6 Form-combo enchants (Overdraw/Linger/Radiance/Execute/Coiled/Siphon), each of which is literally inert unless you wove that Form — and they live in exactly two of the eight slots' pools with no way to target them except paying 45–260 material to re-roll blind. Meanwhile the game's own "is this an upgrade?" signal (PowerRating) is blind to traits, skill rate, haul, crit, damage-%, and every enchant, so the EQUIP BEST button and the green ▲ badge rank 6 of the 8 slots purely on whether they happened to roll a Defense affix.

**Real choice available:** Two real decisions exist — "which Form-combo enchant matches my woven Forms" and "do I churn Essence on this trait" — and both are buried under an EQUIP BEST button whose ranking function cannot see either of them; everything else is rarity-sorting a bag.

**Designed but not reachable:**
- SPLINTER is fully dead. SoloBattle.cs:188 calls bonus.AddQuality(0.15f) → SoloExpedition.cs:191 folds it into Haul.Quality → Game1.cs:1237-1239 credits only Haul.Gleam and Haul.Cores. Nothing anywhere reads Haul.Quality. It is 1 of the 3 enchants in the WEAPON pool, so a third of all weapon enchant rolls (and every Essence/Core spent re-rolling into it) do literally nothing. Two keystones with Rarity: 2.0 (Keystones.cs:74,113) are dead for the same reason.
- Item ELEMENT never reaches combat. LootSystem.cs sets it from the region, MergeRecipe.cs:130-136 carries it through merges, and the only reads are display strings (ItemNaming.cs:97, ForgeScreen.cs:857,964, CharacterScreen.cs:380-388) plus a cosmetic 'SOURCE FOCUS 50%' row (CharacterScreen.cs:231-234). The class docs on LootSystem.cs:24-28 explicitly justify hoarding by element for merges — and the merged item's element is itself only ever displayed. The whole axis is a closed loop with no payoff.
- PowerRating / PowerContribution is the game's only 'is this better' signal and is blind to traits, SkillRate, Haul, Crit and every enchant (Gear.cs:114, HunterProgression.cs:148,162-172). EQUIP BEST (CharacterScreen.cs:183-184) therefore actively mis-solves 6 of 8 slots, ranking them by Defense affix only. The doc comment at HunterProgression.cs:153-160 claims this is the correct number to compare with.
- Greater Refine is an unbounded exploit: 1 Crystal for +5 item levels (Forge.cs:224-233), 80 Crystal from one dismantled Legendary (Forge.cs:31 × LootSystem.cs:111), and no ItemLevel cap anywhere. ItemLevelFactor (Gear.cs:91) and the affix ilvlFactor (ItemAffixes.cs:76) are both linear and uncapped.
- Merge destroys everything the player invested in the inputs. Forge.cs:138 mints a new InstanceId, so trait, enchant and all affixes re-roll; TraitOverride/EnchantOverride are silently dropped. The merge PREVIEW (ForgeScreen.cs:1235-1238) shows only type and element, so it advertises determinism for the two axes nobody cares about while the three that matter are a blind gamble. MergeRecipe.cs:14-25 argues at length that a rolled output would make the Forge a slot machine.
- Trait pool collapse: 6 of the 8 slots (Focus, Helm, Chest, Gloves, Boots, Ring) share one 4-trait pool via the `_ =>` fallthrough at GearTraits.cs:53. Five armour/jewellery slots have no identity of their own. Enchantments.cs:148-154 splits the same slots differently (Ring goes with Charm), so a Ring's trait pool and enchant pool disagree about what a Ring is.

### Currencies and sinks: Gleam, Scrap/Essence/Core/Crystal, Mastery, Dust

Eight numeric resources exist (Gleam, Scrap, Essence, Core, Crystal, Mastery-the-tree-point, Mastery-the-Warren-pool, Memory Dust, plus dead creature Cores), but only three are on the HUD — Scrap, Dust, Gleam (Game1.cs:1671-1673). In actual play the economy is one system: the Warren facility dashboard, which mints all three headline currencies passively at rates 3-4 orders of magnitude above every other faucet and every sink, and is the only place currency is ever a real constraint. The salvage tiers are elegantly designed on paper — one material per rarity band, one verb per material (Material.cs:12-20) — but Scrap's high-volume sink is strictly dominated by Crystal's, Essence/Core/Crystal never appear outside one Forge sub-screen, and the two largest authored sinks (Gleam→stat training, 79,578 lifetime capacity; Scrap→creature Feed) have no player-reachable call site at all. Memory Dust and Mastery are nominally distinct progression currencies but are both produced by the same Warren facilities, in quantities that buy out their entire finite trees (1,775 Dust / 42 nodes) in minutes. The player's only recurring currency decision is which of eight Warren facilities to upgrade next, and even that is single-variable because Gleam is the only cost that ever binds.

**Real choice available:** Almost none — one recurring decision (which Warren facility to upgrade) with one binding cost (Gleam), inside a system that prints that Gleam faster than any sink can drain it.

**Designed but not reachable:**
- GLEAM'S HEADLINE SINK IS UNREACHABLE. Hunter.Train() has zero player-facing call sites: the only calls in src/ are inside the RH_SHOT_MODE screenshot-fixture block (Game1.cs:761-775, guarded at Game1.cs:653) and the save-restore replay (SaveGame.cs:447). StatsScreen.cs:22-23 says outright 'stat TRAINING (hunter.Train) is intentionally not here — flagged for the owner, since it currently has no other home.' SoloExpeditionScreen.cs:260 says it 'moved to the CHARACTER screen (press C)'; CharacterScreen has only Loadout/Equipped/Inventory/Detail panels (CharacterScreen.cs:57-61). The help screen still tells the player 'SPEND GLEAM ON COMMAND TO STRENGTHEN YOUR CHAMPION' (Game1.cs:1813) and the key legend still reads 'V — STATS — TRAIN' (Game1.cs:1783). There is no COMMAND screen anywhere in the source. All 9 stats DO feed combat (SoloBattle.cs:136,157,159,161; HunterProgression.cs:233,238,263,269) — they are simply frozen at base rank forever.
- SCRAP'S FEED SINK IS UNREACHABLE. Feed spends 10 Scrap into a creature's evolution (AutomationScreen.cs:257,272), but AutomationScreen is dormant — Game1.cs:147-148: 'The old creature den (_automation) is retired from the UI; _automation is kept only to carry the saved roster/cores forward.' ForgeScreen.cs:1189 confirms 'FEED was retired'. Nav slot 5 is labelled WARREN but routes to the facility dashboard (Game1.cs:1516). Scrap's only live sink is REFINE, which Greater Refine dominates.
- CREATURE CORES ARE A DEAD CURRENCY. Boss waves mint 2*haul Cores (SoloExpedition.cs:186), credited to _automation.Cores (Game1.cs:1240). The only consumer is Hatch() on the dormant AutomationScreen (AutomationScreen.cs:226-227). They accumulate forever, are saved, and are never spendable. They are not shown on the HUD.
- THE ENTIRE REGION-FARM FAUCET IS DEAD ON A FRESH SAVE. Region.Assign() is only reachable from the dormant AutomationScreen (AutomationScreen.cs:213-214) and from save restore (Game1.cs:371,379). SeedNewGame creates 3 starter creatures and assigns none (Game1.cs:468-478) while its comment claims 'The starter creatures staff the WARREN now (the idle farm)'. TickFarms skips any region with Team.Count == 0 (Game1.cs:530), so kills, cores, the 24g/hr auto-sell and work-tick RMP all produce zero, permanently.
- SHARPENED RECALL I-IV (130 Dust) BUY NOTHING IN PRACTICE. DustEffects.MasteryRate (DustEffects.cs:69-76) is passed only into Region.Tick's masteryRate parameter (Game1.cs:420,532), which multiplies work-tick RMP — unreachable per the above. The reachable RMP path, RecordActiveKill (RegionAutomation.cs:186, called at Game1.cs:1241), does not apply masteryRate at all.
- 90 DUST OF NODES STILL DO NOTHING. HUNTER'S LEDGER (20), FORGE INSIGHT (25) and COMPLETE ATTUNEMENT (45) are 'wired' only in the sense that DustEffects.ShowExactNumbers / ShowMergePreview / TreeComplete exist (DustEffects.cs:157-175); grep shows zero call sites outside DustEffects.cs. The catalog's own comment (MemoryDust.cs:208-213) claims 'tests assert every id here is reachable from DustEffects' — the test checks a hand-maintained NamedWires string set (DustEffects.cs:185-192), not that anything consumes the return value. This is the exact failure mode the class was written to prevent, recurring.

### Regions, conquest, corruption ladder and region mastery automation

Six hardcoded regions in a strict linear chain (Regions.cs:38-69). You "conquer" one by letting your auto-battling champion reach wave 7 in it (Game1.cs:1277, 1318) — there is no boss fight, no objective, no choice; the champion fights on a timer while you watch or don't. Conquest is a one-shot latch that unlocks the next region, pays 40 Memory Dust, +5 mastery-tree points, and +10% Warren production; regions differ only by three numbers (a ladder index multiplier, an attack-tempo enum, and a flat health/damage/loot-tier modifier record). Once all six are conquered a DEEPEN button appears that increments a global corruption tier — an uncapped, cost-free, no-confirmation ratchet that pays 60×tier Dust per press and multiplies enemy HP and damage by (1+0.6×tier). The entire "region mastery automation" half of this system — RMP accrual from creature work-ticks, the four-role composition puzzle, the 25/40→100/120% idle-efficiency bands, the auto-sell cap, the per-region farm teams — is fully implemented in Core and **completely unreachable in the shipping build**: `AutomationScreen` is constructed (Game1.cs:596) and never `Update()`d or `Draw()`n (the A/WARREN nav slot draws the facility dashboard instead, Game1.cs:1119-1125, 1516), so no creature can ever be assigned to a farm, so `TickFarms` skips every region forever (Game1.cs:530).

**Real choice available:** Almost none: the only choices are which unlocked region to sit in (a Source-matchup and a ×1.1–1.25 stat twist) and how many times to press DEEPEN — and the automation/composition layer that was supposed to carry the depth is completely unreachable code.

**Designed but not reachable:**
- **AutomationScreen is never Updated or Drawn.** It is constructed at Game1.cs:596 and its `Update()`/`Draw()` have zero callers (the A/WARREN nav slot routes to `DrawWarren()` — the facility dashboard — at Game1.cs:1119-1125 and 1516). Consequences: no creature can ever be assigned to a region farm; `TickFarms` hits `if (farm.Team.Count == 0) continue;` (Game1.cs:530) for all six regions forever; the entire RegionAutomation.Tick body, the four role stations, the RMP-per-work-tick formula, IdleEfficiencyPercent, AutomationClearTimeSeconds, the auto-sell cap and the AutomationStage 1/2/3 selector are dead in play. This is the single biggest gap in the system.
- Creature cores accumulate with no sink. `_automation.Cores` is credited from every wave (Game1.cs:1240) and from offline (Game1.cs:428), is saved as `UnhatchedCores`, and the only consumer is the private `Hatch()` inside the unreachable AutomationScreen (AutomationScreen.cs:224-233). The player accrues a currency they can never spend.
- `MasteryLevel.OptimizedTeam` (the 100-120% idle band, and regionProgression = 4) is arithmetically unreachable because it requires `CompositionCompleteness() >= 1f` (RegionAutomation.cs:169), which requires an assigned team, which requires the dead screen. The top rung of the mastery ladder does not exist.
- The GDD's four Automation Capability Stages are supposed to be gated on mastery level (design/gdd/region-mastery-automation-system.md A5: Stage 3 requires FullyMastered, Stage 4 requires Optimized). In code `AutomationStage` is a plain settable int with a free +/- button, no cost and no gate (RegionAutomation.cs:105; AutomationScreen.cs:306-307, 329-330). "Earned, not given" is a comment, not a rule.
- `region_storage_capacity` (GDD A7, default 500/resource), the Blocked/Starved work-state signals, and Stage 4 offline progression's closed-form saturation model (GDD Formula 8) are entirely absent from Core. Offline is a flat `farm.Tick(credited)` replay (Game1.cs:420) with no capacity constraint.
- The GDD specifies the Stage-3 Gleam cap as GLOBAL and account-wide (A8, explicitly to prevent multiplying it by mastering more regions). The code makes it per-`Region` instance (RegionAutomation.cs:33, 301-313) — exactly the failure mode A8 was written to prevent, and the world now ships six regions.

### Warren facilities: what they produce, their costs and their curves

The Warren is a flat list of 8 always-unlocked facilities, each with exactly one number that changes: its level. Every facility is mechanically identical — level × authored base rate × an additive milestone bonus, feeding one of three currencies — and every facility shares the *same* upgrade cost curve (5300×1.50^L gleam / 43×1.25^L mastery / 22×1.25^L dust), so the only thing distinguishing them is the base rate constant. Production runs every frame on every screen (Game1.cs:538-545) and offline up to 24h, crediting real Gleam/Dust balances and a Mastery pool that is added straight into the mastery-tree point total. In play it is a two-verb screen: click a card, click UPGRADE, repeat; there is no timer, no queue, no adjacency, no capacity, no unlock, and no per-facility mechanic. Because cost grows 1.5× per level while output grows linearly, payback goes from 0.3h at L1→2 to 353h at L20→21 — the system self-terminates around facility L15-20 and the four non-Gleam facilities are finished at level 1.

**Real choice available:** Almost none — 8 facilities, 1 shared cost curve, 1 verb, and the optimal order is fixed by 8 authored constants; the only real decision (gleam vs mastery vs dust) resolves permanently within ~3 minutes because both non-gleam sinks are finite and tiny.

**Designed but not reachable:**
- GDD/code drift on the headline cost curve: design/gdd/warren-facilities.md §4 states Gleam cost `18700 × 1.40^L`; the code is `5300 × 1.50^L` (Warren.cs:64-65). Different base AND different growth — every cost number in the GDD is wrong.
- GDD §4 omits the milestone system entirely — it documents output as `round(BaseRate × L)` while the code is `round(BaseRate × L × (1 + 0.15 × (L/5)))` (Warren.cs:118, :115). The milestone feature is implemented and displayed but undocumented in the design doc's formula section.
- Offline production silently drops the conquest bonus. The offline catch-up tick runs at Game1.cs:391, but `_warren.ConqueredRegions` is only ever assigned at Game1.cs:540 and Game1.cs:558 — both after load. `Warren.Restore` (Warren.cs:256-263) does not set it and it defaults to 0. So up to 24h of offline production is credited at ConquestBonus = 0, losing up to +80% (8 regions × 0.10) of the multiplier at Warren.cs:197.
- Integer overflow makes late upgrades free. `UpgradeCost` casts to int (Warren.cs:133) but 5300×1.50^32 = 2,286,631,381 > int.MaxValue. At facility level 32 the gleam cost goes negative; `CanAfford` (Warren.cs:209-213) then passes trivially, `SpendGleam` rejects amount<=0 and takes nothing (src/ResonanceHunter.Core/Economy/HunterProgression.cs:296-297), yet Game1.cs:572 calls `Upgrade` anyway — free gleam upgrades from L32 up. Separately, Gleam is itself an int (HunterProgression.cs:73) capping the balance at 2.147B, so L31 (1,524,420,921) is the last level the player can even bank for.
- Designed but unreachable: `Facility.OnMilestone` (Warren.cs:112) and `WarrenYield.Any` (Warren.cs:49) are dead — never referenced anywhere in src/ or tests/.
- The CREATURES sub-view is documented but does not exist. GDD §3 and the WarrenScreen doc-comment (WarrenScreen.cs:15) both say the creature den is 'preserved as a sub-view reached via the CREATURES button'; there is no such button in WarrenScreen.cs, and Game1.cs:935 comments 'deprecated: the creature den was removed'.

### Idle/offline earnings and automation — what happens while away

In play, "idle" is three unrelated faucets and only one of them matters. The dominant one is the Warren facility economy: eight facilities that exist at level 1 from the first frame and immediately pay ~1,816 Gleam + 243 Mastery + 563 Dust per minute, on every screen and while the game is closed, with no gate, no unlock order, and no build timers. The second is the champion auto-battler, which pays per cleared wave live and at a flat 50% of its measured gleam/second offline. The third — the region/creature farm with its composition puzzle (CCS/PQS), mastery ladder, idle-efficiency bands and auto-sell cap — is fully implemented in Core and fully unit-tested, but is completely unreachable in the shipped build: the only code that assigns a creature to a region lives in AutomationScreen, which is never Update()d or Draw()n, so every farm's team is empty and every region tick returns on the first branch. The only real offline decision is when to come back, and the welcome-back toast doesn't even report the income that actually landed.

**Real choice available:** One real decision — "click UPGRADE on whichever Gleam facility is cheapest" — repeated until the 1.5^level cost curve outruns the linear income curve; the entire designed idle system (composition, roles, mastery ladder, automation stages) is unreachable dead code.

**Designed but not reachable:**
- AutomationScreen.cs (544 lines: 4 role stations, hatchery, pen, feed/evolve, stage controls) has zero Update() or Draw() call sites. Game1.cs:1119-1125 explicitly no-ops it and Game1.cs:1516 routes the WARREN nav to DrawWarren() instead. Game1.cs:935 admits it: 'deprecated: the creature den was removed'.
- Consequence: `region.Assign()` is only reachable from AutomationScreen.cs:214 and the save-restore path (Game1.cs:371, 379). A fresh game seeds 3 starter creatures (Game1.cs:468-473) but never assigns them, so `farm.Team.Count == 0` for every region and TickFarms skips every farm at Game1.cs:530 — forever. RegionAutomation.cs (315 lines) never executes past that guard in a real session.
- Consequently dead in play: the CCS/PQS/TeamQuality composition math, all four idle-efficiency bands, the 500/2000/5000 mastery ladder as an idle mechanic, the 24 Gleam/hr auto-sell cap, per-creature work-tick accrual, and the AutomationStage 1/2/3 progression. All of it is green under tests (tests/unit/.../RegionAutomationTests.cs, tests/integration/.../FullLoopTests.cs:84-88) because the tests call the model directly, bypassing the missing UI.
- Cores are produced (2 per boss wave, SoloExpedition.cs:186; 1 per farm kill, RegionAutomation.cs:277), accumulated in `_automation.Cores`, and persisted as UnhatchedCores (SaveGame.cs:39) — but Hatch() is only reachable from the dead screen (AutomationScreen.cs:224-233), and cores are not even shown in the currency pills (Game1.cs:1661-1668). A pure write-only counter.
- Creature evolution is credited every wave and every chest (EvolutionCredit at Game1.cs:1248, 1263) against a roster the player can never see, expand, feed, or evolve.
- The help screen still advertises the removed feature: Game1.cs:1787 reads ("A", "WARREN — HATCH"). WarrenScreen.cs:15-16 claims the creature den is 'preserved as a sub-view reached via the CREATURES button' — there is no such button anywhere in the file.

## Where the systems fight each other

### [high] The Warren out-produces every other faucet by 3–4 orders of magnitude, so no currency is ever a constraint — including the one the trait tree will cost

At level 1, before any upgrade, the eight facilities mint 1,780 Gleam/min, 240 Mastery/min and 558 Dust/min (Warren.cs:38-45, credited Game1.cs:541-543). The entire 1,775-Dust tree is affordable after ~3.2 minutes of idling; the entire 42-node mastery tree after ~10 seconds. Offline production runs at full rate capped at 24h (SaveGame.cs:471), so one night away yields ~270,000 Dust — 152x the whole tree. Every other faucet is a rounding error against this: conquest pays 40 Dust, a region-mastery level pays 15, a corruption deepening pays 60, and the region-farm auto-sell is hard-capped at 24 Gleam/hour across all six regions (RegionAutomation.cs:33) with a doc-comment claiming that cap 'defuses a genuine inflation bomb'. MemoryDust.cs:95-97 still asserts 'This is the ONLY faucet: Dust comes from mastering regions' and a test is literally named test_the_only_way_to_gain_dust_is_mastery — while ~99.9% of real income arrives from FORAGING PITS. Consequence for the redesign: converting prestige into a permanent trait tree does not make it a progression track, because it is not paced by anything the player does in the game. It is paced by wall-clock idle time on a system that started fully switched on. The flow redesign must decide, once, whether the trait tree is bought with idle-time currency or with play-depth currency — right now it is nominally the latter and actually the former, and the Warren also silently funds the mastery tree and its own upgrades from the same faucets.

### [high] Removing the reset makes the Dust tree and the Mastery tree the same object — three permanent trees already multiply the same five numbers

MasteryTree and MemoryDustTree both terminate in the identical `BuildMods(Damage, Health, SkillRate, Haul, Rarity)` record (Build.cs:29-33), combined multiplicatively through the same `BuildMods.Combine` in `PlayerLoadout.ToBuild`. Gear traits and affixes feed the same five floats a third time. Composition: 36 of 42 spendable mastery nodes are flat +6%..+18% on those axes (MasteryTree.cs:80-119); 15 of 44 Dust nodes are flat +8%/+10% on the same axes, and every one of them is one of exactly five values (MemoryDust.cs:229,239,257,270,347). Today the two trees have exactly ONE structural difference: mastery respec is free, instant and unlimited (MasteryTree.cs:226-230) while Dust is permanent and unrefundable (MemoryDust.cs:126-133). That permanence was the entire justification for a separate currency, a separate screen, a separate node graph and a separate save field. Reframing prestige as 'a permanent trait tree' deletes the last distinguishing property and leaves two trees that grant the same mods, in the same magnitudes, in different currencies, on different screens, one of which you can undo. The redesign has to either merge them or give each a genuinely different verb — the honest split available is that mastery grants NUMBERS and the Dust tree grants VOCABULARY (keystones, Vows, triggers), which is roughly what the code half-does already and never commits to.

### [high] The prestige hole is not the reset (there was never one) — it is that the Dust tree was the game's only irreversible decision and its only content drip, and neither survives becoming a flat trait tree

There is no prestige reset in the codebase: grep for Rebirth/Ascend/PrestigeReset/HardReset/WipeProgress returns zero hits, the DUST screen's 'RESET' button resets the category FILTER (PrestigeScreen.cs:206), and CorruptionScaling.cs:12-13 states the design pillar outright — 'a ONE-WAY ratchet that resets NOTHING (the game's prestige pillar)'. So removing the reset removes nothing mechanical. What the Dust tree WAS doing, and now needs a new home, is four separate jobs: (1) It is the sole gate on all build vocabulary — before vow_study_1 (15 Dust) the Vow axis literally does not exist (PlayerLoadout.CycleVow's option list is `[null]`), and each of the 13 keystones exists only behind its own 60-Dust gate. This is the game's only drip-feed of new verbs. (2) It is the ONLY unrefundable choice in the entire game. Mastery respec is free, the loadout is free to re-cycle, gear swaps are free, region travel is free, and death is free (a wipe mints a brand-new full-health champion at wave 1 with every wave's rewards already banked, SoloExpeditionScreen.cs:272-282). Strip the permanence and the game contains zero commitments — every choice is a toggle. (3) It is the only reason conquest, region mastery and corruption pay anything at all (Game1.cs:1280, 1085; CorruptionScaling.cs:32) — remove Dust's meaning and the whole world layer's reward column is empty. (4) It is the post-finish-line track corruption exists to feed ('so the permanent Memory Dust economy keeps moving'). Jobs (1) and (2) are precisely the ones a flat multiplier tree cannot carry: 31 of the 44 nodes are already an obviously-correct shopping list with no refusal in it.

### [high] Depth is quantized to multiples of 5 and gated almost entirely by one stat, so most investment produces literally zero observable progress

Depth is the single number every system feeds and is scored on. But the boss multiplier of 2.2 is applied to enemy DAMAGE as well as health (SoloExpedition.cs:136 passes the same `scale` to both parameters; WaveModel.cs:25's comment only claims health), which makes every 5th wave a wall. Measured across a Python re-implementation of SoloBattle.ResolveWave: 20 of 21 sampled runs across the whole training range died on a boss wave. Depth therefore moves in steps of 5 and nothing else — stat ranks 21/24/27 all produce depth 44; ranks 45/48/51 all produce depth 64. Worse, at starter power the binding constraint is the 100 HP pool, not damage: 4x Projectile, 3x Projectile+Mark, 2 Projectile/1 Strike/1 Transformation, 4x Transformation and the starter build ALL reach exactly depth 19. So the two things the player spends the most time configuring — the loadout and the damage side of both trees — change the score by zero across enormous ranges, and the feedback arrives as a staircase with the risers hidden. Compounding it, `_regionProgression` makes a farmed region strictly harder (+35% HP / +20% damage per level, Game1.cs:1228-1229) with no combat-side compensation, so sustained farming can visibly LOSE the player a boss wall. Any flow redesign that keeps depth as the score has to unquantize it or stop scoring on it.

### [high] The mastery tree gives away for free the exact triggers the Dust keystones sell with a permanent tax — and the build screen can only ever wear three keystones anyway

BuildTrigger is a shared vocabulary, held as a HashSet (Build.cs:307), so the same trigger from two sources is one trigger. Mastery notables grant them with `BuildMods.None` — zero cost (MasteryTree.cs:154). morph_n grants Echo ('EVERY SKILL FIRES TWICE') for 3 mastery points, which the game gives away by the dozen; the ECHO keystone sells the identical trigger for 60 Dust AND a permanent Damage x0.6 (Keystones.cs:61). strike_n grants Bloodlust free; the BLOODLUST keystone charges Health x0.75 (Keystones.cs:54). Socketing the keystone after taking the notable is a pure, silent, uncompensated penalty. And nothing gates breadth: `CanTake` blocks only a second Mastery corner (MasteryTree.cs:209), points exceed node count by mid-game, so every finished character carries all six notables — Bloodlust + Overdraw + Radiance + Desperation + Linger + Echo simultaneously — and the 'which arm are you' question the tree's own doc comment poses is never asked. On top of that, BuildScreen.cs:170 and :421 both loop `i < learned.Count && i < 3` with no scrolling, so only the first three keystones in catalog order are ever drawn or clickable: learn a fourth and it is permanently unreachable. The one genuine refusal in the whole build system — 3 sockets from a menu of 13, which Build.cs:204-222 argues at length is the point — is deleted by the UI.

### [high] Gleam's designed headline sink is unreachable, so all nine character stats are frozen and Hunter Level is permanently 1 while four screens display it as if it moves

`Hunter.Train()` has zero player-facing call sites. The only calls in src/ are inside the RH_SHOT_MODE screenshot-fixture block (Game1.cs:761-775) and the save-restore replay (SaveGame.cs:447). StatsScreen.Update() is an empty method body (StatsScreen.cs:60) and the class doc admits it: 'stat TRAINING (hunter.Train) is intentionally not here — flagged for the owner, since it currently has no other home.' SoloExpeditionScreen.cs:260 says it 'moved to the CHARACTER screen (press C)'; CharacterScreen has no such panel. The help text still reads 'SPEND GLEAM ON COMMAND TO STRENGTHEN YOUR CHAMPION' (Game1.cs:1813) and the key legend still reads 'V — STATS — TRAIN' (Game1.cs:1783), pointing at a COMMAND screen that does not exist in the source. The stats themselves ARE wired to combat (SoloBattle.cs:136,157,159,161; HunterProgression.cs:233,238,263,269) — they are just pinned at base forever: AttackPower 10, Defense 0 (i.e. 0% mitigation from the stat), CriticalChance 5% giving a flat +3% expected skill damage for the life of the save. 79,556 Gleam of authored lifetime sink (HunterProgression.cs:324-328) vanished from the economy with no replacement, and ~12 unit tests still pass green over the dead feature. DamageBench — the live-sim instrument explicitly built 'so a stat that quietly reaches no formula shows up plainly' — was removed from the UI by the same commit and now has zero non-test call sites. The flow redesign must decide whether character stats come back or die properly; today they are a fifth power source that exists, is displayed, and cannot be touched.

### [medium] Warren mastery and mastery-tree points are one variable with two spenders, and spending it in one place silently steals from the other

`_mastery.SetEarned(3 + _deepestEver / 5 + _world.ConqueredIds.Count * 5 + (int)_warrenMasteryPool)` (Game1.cs:1274) folds the Warren's spendable balance directly into the build tree's derived point total, while `_warrenMasteryPool -= c.Mastery` on a facility upgrade (Game1.cs:570) spends the same pool. The Warren screen labels it 'MASTERY' (WarrenScreen.cs:66); the Build screen labels the identical number 'POINTS' (BuildScreen.cs:378); nothing in either UI indicates they are the same number. Two consequences: buying a facility silently removes build-tree points the player already counted on, and because `Available` clamps at 0 rather than un-taking nodes (MasteryTree.cs:178), the strictly-correct play is to buy the whole tree first and then dump the pool into facilities — you keep both. There is also a real precision hazard: `_warrenMasteryPool` is a long cast to int for SetEarned, and at high facility levels (~1.8M mastery/hr around L50) that overflows after roughly a thousand hours of accumulated idle time.

### [medium] Rarity is a full build axis with a source and no sink, which makes several purchases strictly negative

`BuildMods.Rarity` is one of the five axes the trait tree can grant. It is computed all the way through the sim, folded into `Haul.Quality` (SoloExpedition.cs:191) — and then nothing reads it. Game1.cs:1237-1239 credits only Haul.Gleam and Haul.Cores; DropBossChest rolls loot tier from region ladder / progression / depth / corruption and ignores Haul.Quality entirely; Chest.cs:184 derives quality from the chest's own rarity. Everything feeding that axis is therefore a pure cost: FORTUNE is -25% damage for nothing (Keystones.cs:113), DISCERNING EYE is -50% haul for nothing (Keystones.cs:74), the fortune_1/fortune_2 Dust nodes (45 Dust) buy nothing, SPLINTER's per-kill AddQuality(0.15) is discarded (SoloBattle.cs:188) which makes REAPER (-25% skill rate to grant Splinter) a pure downside, and SPLINTER is 1 of only 3 enchants in the entire WEAPON pool. The wiring test cannot catch this: DustEffects.WiredIds auto-passes any node that merely carries a Mods field (DustEffects.cs:194-200), so 'wired' means 'DustEffects reads it', not 'anything downstream consumes it'. One fifth of the trait tree's expressive vocabulary is inert, and three nodes (HUNTER'S LEDGER 20, FORGE INSIGHT 25, COMPLETE ATTUNEMENT 45) have no game-code consumer at all.

### [medium] The only 'is this better?' number in the game is blind to most of what makes an item better, and it drives the auto-equip button

`PowerRating = round(damageMult * attackPower * 2 + defense * 2 + maxHealth * 0.5)` (Gear.cs:113-114) reads exactly three things: the weapon's rarity/ilvl multiplier, the charm's Defense/HP, and Defense affixes. It never reads WornMods (every GearTrait — HEAVY, SWIFT, WILD, FOCUSED…), nor the Damage / Health / SkillRate / Haul / Crit affixes, nor any of the 11 enchants. So for Helm, Chest, Gloves, Boots, Ring and Focus — six of eight slots — PowerContribution is exactly 0 unless the item happened to roll a Defense affix, a 1-in-6 uniform draw. EQUIP BEST (CharacterScreen.cs:177-187), the green upgrade triangle (CharacterScreen.cs:339) and the Forge 'UP' badge (ForgeScreen.cs:1117-1119) all rank on it, so the game's own convenience button will actively downgrade a player who trusts it, discarding a Legendary with four damage affixes and an Overdraw enchant in favour of a Rare with one Defense roll. The same number is printed on the BUILD screen as 'BUILD POWER' (BuildScreen.cs:61,225), where skills, Vows, keystones and the entire mastery tree move it by exactly zero — the one number on the build screen is structurally blind to the build. A flow redesign that adds a fourth power source without fixing this adds a fourth thing the player cannot see the effect of.

### [medium] Three difficulty tracks stack on the same enemies while the world itself is consumed in minutes, and the endgame ratchet is a free unlimited button

Enemy strength is the product of three independent 'the world gets harder' tracks that no screen reconciles: the region ladder index (x1.35 HP / x1.25 dmg per step), region progression from farming (x1.35 HP / x1.20 dmg per level, Game1.cs:1228-1229), and the corruption tier (+60% HP per tier, CorruptionScaling.cs:18). Two of them are things the player does deliberately and one is a side effect of farming — and only the corruption track pays a scaled reward. Meanwhile the world layer they scale is tiny: conquest fires at wave 7 (Game1.cs:1318), which is one boss plus two trash waves, so all six regions fall in a few minutes of watching, after which the map's only remaining interaction is a DEEPEN button that is repeatable immediately, unlimited, free, and has no confirmation dialog (MapScreen.cs:104,310). Travelling to another region nulls the run and zeroes Deepest (SoloExpeditionScreen.cs:247-252), so the map's one real decision — stay or move — is priced as a total loss of your current depth against a loot-tier bump you cannot see. And the entire authored 'region mastery automation' half of the world layer (RMP work-ticks, the four-role composition puzzle, idle-efficiency bands, per-region farm teams, the 130-Dust SHARPENED RECALL line) is unreachable: AutomationScreen is constructed but never Update()d or Draw()n, so no creature is ever assigned, so TickFarms skips every region forever (Game1.cs:530). The world is simultaneously over-scaled and under-populated.
