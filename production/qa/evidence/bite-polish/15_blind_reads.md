## The attack, everything off (s2)

> "**A bite.** The front creature's jaw dropped open and I saw pale teeth flash in a dark mouth, then it pushed its head
> forward at the champion… the mouth went from a closed dark blob with two white eyes to a gaping jaw with visible
> white/lilac teeth for roughly a third of a second, then closed again. That was the most readable part of the whole
> attack." Order seen: "sat crouched… opened its jaws wide and held them open… stretched its body forward toward
> the champion… snapped back into the crouch with the mouth closed." One attacker: "the front one with the teeth is
> clearly 'the one'". But: "the body barely travels, so it reads as 'snapped its jaws' more than 'lunged and bit'";
> and the champion: "no flinch, no lean back, no step". Confidence medium.

This is the first read in three studies that names the verb.

## Reading it

- **The verb is there.** With everything off, the reader called it a bite and described the maw opening, thrusting and
  closing, and named the front creature as the attacker. The maw did what the pose work alone could not.
- **The travel is still under-read on paper** ("barely travels", "stayed in place"), though the same readers describe
  "pushed its head forward", "darted a step", "stretched forward". The lunge is 80 px at play size over 220 ms; on a
  flipbook that is a few pixels per frame.
- **The recoil is not read on paper at any size tested.** Whether 27 px over 33 ms reads at true speed is the owner's
  call; the flipbook cannot answer it.
- **A reads as automatic in 3 of 6 views, as caused in 2 of 6, and never as a mouth.** Its failures are the same
  sentence every time: "I never saw the creature attack." When the reader sees the bite, A reads as the answer to it;
  when the reader does not, A reads as the Seeker's next turn. The chest-edge mark removed the "burst at his hand,
  he fired" read that the belt-height mark produced.
- Concept A's rough thread still starts AT the Seeker's chest spark, which is the one thing that lets it read as
  "he fired": in the two reads where A failed, the line "from his hand/chest" was the evidence.
