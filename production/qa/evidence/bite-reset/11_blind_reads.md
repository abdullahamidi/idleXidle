neutral file names, no knowledge of the design. As in every battery here, flipbook readers read SHAPES well and

lunge and a fair signal about the marks; the owner's true-speed eye is the test.

**What this says.**
- The warped strip has genuinely different poses (a reader with no prompt separated crouch / rise / peak / settle),
  but the verb they make is REARING, not a head-first lunge: the CONTACT pose is a raised head dipping, not a body
  driven into the target. That is the limit of column warps on a 512 px canvas whose rest head already sits at x 39;
  the honest answer is the brief's own: a redrawn attack strip for COMMIT and CONTACT.
- The motif is SEEN on paper (twice, by readers who missed the lunge), as "a small red curved claw/slash mark". It
  reads as a mark of a hit at his edge; it does not yet say BITE on its own at half size. At play size it is ~40 px
  and closes over two frames; the true-speed film (`03_*`) is where it should be judged.
- The A2 answer is seen ("a purple squiggle by the lead creature") but not connected to the bite by a flipbook
  reader; the echo needs motion to read, so `07_*` / `09c_*` at true speed are the test, not this.
