# JAWS readable-clamp pass: the blind checks (diagnostic only)

Each reader was a FRESH agent with no project context. It saw one image, under a neutral file name, and was told to
read only that file. The prompts never say trap, jaws or chain.

**The owner's rule applies:** an AI reader is a diagnostic, not the authority. True-speed footage and the owner's eye
are the test. What the readers did do is catch two real problems:
- a mouth reading;
- a hidden jaw, fixed before these films.

## 1. Three silhouettes through the motion (`20`)

Each silhouette showed four moments at play size: OPEN, MID, CONTACT, and shut on a limb. The question: "What object is
this? Pick the TWO best words from HOOK, CLAW, GRAPPLING HOOK, PINCER, TRAP, BEAR TRAP, CLAMP, MOUTH. What happens
across the pictures?"

| Candidate | First impression | Two words | The motion, as read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A, the upright bear trap** (short base, two pins close together, broad "( )" jaws) | "a trap-like jaw — looks like a bear trap" | **BEAR TRAP, CLAMP** | "two curved, toothed arms swing shut around a central post, closing tighter frame by frame until they clamp down on the grey bar" |
| B, an asymmetric mantrap along the chain (upper and lower jaws) | "a hinged jaw trap / clamp" | TRAP, CLAMP | "the curved jaw swings shut … clamped onto the grey bar" |
| C, an upright spring yoke (lune jaws) | "a claw-like trap jaw / clamp" | BEAR TRAP, CLAMP | "two curved fang-like jaws mounted on a stand … snap shut around a grey bar" |

- **A was chosen:** BEAR TRAP comes first, and its motion (a wide cup that springs up to "( )") changes shape the most.
- **B's problem:** jaws above and below, hinged at the back and opening sideways, read as a mouth in our own look. Every
  earlier head and clamp failed the same way.
- **C's problem:** it read as "fang-like jaws on a stand".

## 2. The close, frame by frame, at play size (`11a`, `11b`)

Six consecutive in-game frames at 1:1 (16.7 ms apart), from the first frame that shows the bite. The question: "What is
the grey metal object? Pick the TWO best words. Is a closing motion visible? How many moving parts? In which frame does
it first look closed?"

| Frames | Reader | Object | Two words | The close |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CURRENT hook-like clamp | Opus | "a chained metal clamp or pincer" | CLAMP, PINCER | "**I couldn't see a clear closing snap** … it never looks fully closed" |
| NEW, first draw order (the FAR jaw behind the creature) | Opus | "a bear trap on a chain" | BEAR TRAP, TRAP | the jaw swings up at frame 4, "but it looks like **one jaw rotating upright, not two jaws snapping together**" |
| **NEW, final** (the NEAR jaw behind the creature) | Opus | **"a bear trap (a spring-loaded leg trap on a chain)"** | **BEAR TRAP, TRAP** | "a closing motion is visible, though it happens fast, over about two frames. I see **two moving parts: the left and right jaws** … It first looks closed in frame 5 (frame 4 is nearly closed)" |
| **NEW, final** | Sonnet | "a hinged clamp / vise mouth" | CLAMP, BEAR TRAP | "It closes: visible jaws pivot shut on the arm. Two moving parts … first looks fully closed in frame 5" |

**What changed because of this.** Drawn behind the dark imp, the far jaw vanished: it lies over the creature's body. So
the NEAR jaw goes behind instead. It lies mostly outside the creature, so the limb still crosses a jaw, while the far
jaw stays visible in front. The final films use this order.

**Frame 5 vs frame 4.** Both readers named frame 5 as "fully closed". In fact the jaws reach their 3° stop on frame 4
(+50 ms), and frame 5 is the rebound, 4° more OPEN. Frame 4 is also the frame where the answer's hit flash lights the
creature, and the readers seem to have read that flash frame as a transition. The stop, the clack, the number and the
flash are all on frame 4. If the owner also sees the shut land a frame late, the flash could come one frame after the stop,
so the stop frame shows the jaws unlit (presentation only).

**"Fast, over about two frames".** This is the spring spacing the brief asked for. The first frame after the bite is
still open (11 % of the travel), the next is 44 %, and then SHUT. The owner's true-speed look decides whether it needs
another frame.

**"Vise mouth".** One reader used "mouth" for a vise's jaws. The other named no creature. Neither reading is a hook, a
claw or a pincer.
