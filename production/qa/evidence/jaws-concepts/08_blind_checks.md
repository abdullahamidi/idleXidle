# JAWS concept study: blind reads of true-speed frame strips (2026-09-25)

**Method.** Each reader was a fresh subagent with no project context.
- Each read **one** image with a neutral name (`clip_k2.png` …), in a scratch folder whose path names nothing.
- Each was told only four things:
  - the strip is consecutive frames at 60 fps, about a third of a second;
  - the hooded figure is the player's champion and the dark creatures are enemies;
  - the fight plays itself;
  - "answer as a player would after watching it once at full speed".
- They answered the brief's five questions: what happened, who caused it, who retaliated, what it felt like, and
  cast / trap / counter / decoration. They also answered where the main moment was, and whether anything warned of
  it.

**What the strips hide, and why.** The strips are cut to y 522..900, with no painting over.
- The fight's reflected number reads "-7 JAWS CRITICAL", and the skill bar names JAWS "ON BITE". Either would give
  the answer away.
- The tip of the Seeker's hood is cut at rest.
- The strips start 100 ms before the bite. The creature's wind-up starts about 900 ms before it and is barely visible
  (see *Provocation* below).

**Limitation.** A frame strip is MORE forgiving than true speed, because a reader can study it. A reader saying "I'd
miss it at full speed" is a strong negative. Seeing a thing in a strip is only a weak positive. The owner's eye on
the true-speed films (01, 02, 05–07) is the real test.

## What the reads say

1. **No concept read as a COUNTER, because the provocation does not read.**
   - Every reader, the control included, saw the enemy's bite as a red burst on the hunter "in the same frame". None
     saw an attack from the creature, which barely moves in its last half second (its eight-frame clip crouches in
     place).
   - A retaliation cannot read while the thing it answers is invisible. This is an enemy-presentation fact outside
     JAWS, and it bounds every concept.
2. **CAUSE comes from a line out of the Seeker.**
   - A (its brief thread) and B (the tether) were given to the hunter.
   - C (no line) "just appeared" and "doesn't look like it came from him".
3. **Anticipation of 17–50 ms is not seen when it is dim (A, C). When it is bright (B), it flips the cause.**
   - A's forming crescents and C's telegraph were "very dim / practically invisible at full speed".
   - B's lash leaving before the bite was seen, and it made the hunter the INITIATOR: a cast, "nothing hit the hero
     first".
   - Pre-rolling the manifestation does not buy a "trap was armed" read in 50 ms. Deployed traps get that read from
     SECONDS of presence.
4. **The fight's own white hit flash is the loudest thing on screen.** It is the reflected hit's usual flash, about
   150 ms of full-white body. The readers called it "ghost", "soul knocked out", "the loudest thing". It melted A's
   teeth and "drowned out" C.
5. **A reads as a MOUTH.** "A monster's mouth more than a metal trap." This is the same species of read as the
   rejected crocodile head, now in shadow.
6. **B reads as a hook-and-pull, a cast.**
   - With HARD HANDS it reads as a COMBO: "hook, then dash in and hit".
   - That is visually coherent, but it is a false causal link: the leap was not caused by the tether.
